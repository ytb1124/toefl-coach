Similarly, some species of squid and jellyfish exhibit bioluminescence to escape predators by confusing or startling them.

- (W) In everyday life, people often find themselves adjusting their thoughts or actions based on what others around them are doing. One classic study from the 1950s, led by psychologist Solomon Asch, explored how individuals respond when their own perceptions differ from those of a group. In the experiment, participants were shown a set of lines and asked to identify which one matched a reference line in length. Now let's talk about why this has been of critical importance to people throughout the history of humanity.

### TPO6-L6 — Life Sciences Talk — relationships between organisms (Main Idea)

스크립트:

- (N) Listen to a talk on a life sciences podcast.
- (W) In nature, organisms interact in various ways. Long-term interactions between two different species can be mutualistic, commensalistic, or parasitic. A mutualistic relationship is when it's beneficial for both species. A classic case is the relationship between bees and flowers. Bees collect nectar from flowers for food. As they do so, the bees inadvertently help flowers pollinate. Pollination is the process of transferring plants' reproductive material—the pollen—from plant to plant, which is essential for plant reproduction. Commensalism involves one species benefiting, while the other is neither helped nor harmed. An example is when a type of small sea animal, called a barnacle, attaches itself to whales. The barnacles benefit from this in several ways, such as by catching a ride to places in the ocean where food is abundant. You see, whales feed on microorganisms called plankton by filtering them out of the water. And so do barnacles, which reach plankton-rich areas while being attached to the whale. The barnacles benefit from their relationship with the whale, while the whale remains unaffected. Parasitism occurs when one species benefits at the expense of the other. An example is the relationship between mammals and the insects we call ticks. Ticks feed on a mammal's blood. The blood loss can be significant for the mammal and could potentially cause anemia. Anemia is when the blood has a reduced capacity for carrying oxygen and can cause fatigue and weakness.

**Q.** What is the main topic of the talk?

- A. How organisms protect themselves from other organisms ← 내 답 (빨강)
- B. How environmental changes affect animals' feeding and reproduction
- C. Benefits of studying species in their natural environment
- D. Types of relationships between living organisms ← 정답 (초록)
