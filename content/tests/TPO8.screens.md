## Academic Reading — Industrial Revolution and Its Impact (4번 오답)

The Industrial Revolution, starting in the late eighteenth century, transformed economies and societies. It marked the shift from agrarian to industrial economies, mainly in Europe and North America. This period saw the rise of factories, mechanized production, and technologies like the steam engine. These developments increased production and efficiency but also brought dramatic social changes.

One significant impact of the Industrial Revolution involved urbanization. As factories emerged, people moved from rural areas to cities for work. This migration led to overcrowded cities, poor living conditions, and slums. However, despite these challenges, cities became centers of economic activity and innovation.

Working conditions in factories were often harsh. Workers, including children, faced long hours, low wages, and unsafe environments. [■] This exploitation of workers prompted the rise of labor unions, which fought for better wages, safer conditions, and reasonable hours, leading to labor reforms that improved workers' lives.

The Industrial Revolution also impacted the environment. The increased use of coal and fossil fuels led to pollution and environmental degradation. It sparked innovations in transportation, with railways and steamships facilitating movement. Overall, it set the stage for the modern industrial world, with consequences still felt today.

## Academic Talk — Corporate governance (1·4번 오답)

(N) Listen to a talk in a business class.
(W–Am) Let's talk about corporate governance. Corporate governance is the way a company is governed and managed. It's like a set of rules and practices that help make sure the company runs smoothly and fairly. It's important because it helps balance the needs of groups like shareholders, managers, customers, and suppliers. One key part of corporate governance is the board of directors. This group makes major decisions about where the company is headed. A strong board is often made up of people with diverse backgrounds—for example, individuals with experience in finance, law, operations, or marketing. This mix of expertise helps the board look at issues from different angles and make more informed decisions.
Another important part is ensuring transparency. This means the company should be open and honest about what it's doing and how it's performing. One major way companies show transparency is through financial reports. Companies should regularly share financial reports to show how they're doing financially, and also explain any risks they're facing. When companies are transparent, it builds trust and helps investors feel more confident.
Of course, putting good corporate governance into practice isn't always easy. It takes effort and money—the cost of hiring outside experts to check the company's finances could be high. And you'll need to set up systems in advance to make sure rules are being followed.

## Academic Talk — Coral reefs (4번 오답)

Listen to a talk on a life sciences podcast.
(M–Au) Let's dive into the fascinating world of coral reefs, which provide crucial habitat for a wide variety of fish and other marine organisms. The reefs themselves are created by countless tiny creatures known as coral polyps. Each polyp is a small, soft–bodied animal that lives in colonies and secretes calcium carbonate. This calcium carbonate then forms the hard skeletons that build up coral reefs over time. What's truly remarkable is that coral polyps contain microscopic organisms, called algae, inside the coral's own tissues. These algae transform sunlight into usable energy by performing the process of photosynthesis. This way, the algae provide the coral polyps with most of their energy. So the algae and corals are in a mutually beneficial relationship that fuels the entire reef ecosystem.
But coral reefs are in trouble. One of the most pressing threats they face is coral bleaching. When ocean temperatures rise, as they do today, coral polyps often become stressed and expel the algae living in their tissues. This not only turns the corals ghostly white but also strips them of their main energy source. If the stress continues, the polyps can die, leaving behind a lifeless reef structure and threatening the entire ecosystem that depends on it.Scientists are bringing some coral reefs back to life through a method called coral gardening. Scientists grow healthy coral fragments in underwater nurseries and transplant them to degraded reefs. This hands–on restoration technique helps rebuild reef structures, support marine life, and even select heat–resistant coral strains for future resilience.

## Academic Talk — Crowdfunding (1·2·3번 오답)

Listen to a talk on a business podcast.
(M–Cn) Crowdfunding has become a popular way for entrepreneurs to raise money for their projects. It involves collecting small amounts of money from many people, usually through online platforms. These platforms allow entrepreneurs to bypass traditional funding sources like banks and venture capitalists, giving them direct access to potential customers and investors. What customers can get in exchange is first dibs on the first products of their kind when they're eventually produced. For entrepreneurs, one major benefit is that crowdfunding lowers financial risk. By getting funds through preorders or donations, entrepreneurs can test if people are truly interested in their idea before spending a lot of time and money. And the crowdfunding can create buzz and build a community around a product, which helps with long–term success.
However, there are challenges. Entrepreneurs must put in time and effort to create strong campaigns and keep supporters engaged. Competition is high, and not every project meets its funding goal. Even after raising money, it can be difficult to actually make and deliver the promised product. Entrepreneurs need to keep supporters updated and be honest if problems come up—like delays in manufacturing or shipping the product—so they don't lose trust. For example, there was this high–tech cooler for which millions of dollars were raised. But production delays happened and weren't properly communicated to the financial backers. The project never recovered from its reputational loss and went bankrupt, and many people who paid for the cooler never received one.
