### TPO5-L5 — Economics Talk — behavioral economics (Detail)

스크립트:

- (N) Listen to a talk in an economics class.
- (M) Let's spend some time talking about behavioral economics. This is the branch of economics that considers ways in which psychological factors influence how people make economic decisions. Traditional economics assumes that people are rational actors who make decisions in a way that maximizes their utility—that achieves the greatest possible benefit for themselves. Of course, people are not rational beings, or at least not all of the time. Their choices are often affected by biases and emotions, and these things naturally affect people's economic decisions. A key concept in behavioral economics is "loss aversion." This is the idea that avoiding losses is more important to people than acquiring equivalent gains. Losing money is painful, and getting it is pleasurable, right? But according to loss aversion, losing $50 feels more painful than gaining $50 feels pleasurable. This preference can lead to irrational decision-making, such as holding onto investments that are losing money longer than is financially wise. Another important idea is "anchoring." This is when a person relies heavily on an initial piece of information when making a decision. That initial piece of information is the "anchor." If you see a shirt, say, that costs $100, and then you see it on sale for $50—that sounds like a pretty good deal, right? Even if in your heart of hearts you know that the shirt's not actually worth $50! Behavioral principles like these are crucial for economists, policymakers, and businesses. They help create better products, policies, and interventions by reflecting real human behavior.

**Q.** According to the speaker, one problem with traditional economics is that it

- A. accepts the biases and emotions of ordinary people
- B. forgets that losing money is painful and getting it is pleasurable
- C. favors rational people over those who aim to maximize their benefit ← 내 답 (빨강)
- D. assumes that people will always act in a way that maximizes their utility ← 정답 (초록)
