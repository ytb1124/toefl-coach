## Academic Talk — Linear vs circular economy (1번)

(N) Listen to a talk in an environmental science class.
(W-Au) The traditional economy of production is linear. It follows a “take, make, dispose” model. A typical case is the production of single-use plastic water bottles. Raw materials like crude oil and natural gas are extracted to produce plastic. These materials are then processed and manufactured into plastic bottles and filled with water. After consumption, the bottles are often discarded after a single use. Many end up in landfills or, worse, in the natural environment, where they are a major source of pollution.
A circular economy, on the other hand, is a production system aimed at eliminating waste and promoting the continual use of resources. One goal of the circular economy is designing out waste by keeping products and materials in use. This can be achieved through strategies like remanufacturing or recycling. Take the electronics industry. Companies are developing products with modular designs that can be easily upgraded or repaired, reducing the need for new materials. Additionally, programs for recycling old electronics help recover valuable materials like metals and plastics, which can be used to make new products.
The circular economy can also regenerate land by returning valuable nutrients to the soil. In composting, for example, food scraps, agricultural residues, or other organic waste products are collected and composted, which, uh, is a natural process that uses microorganisms. These microorganisms break down organic matter into nutrient-rich humus. The humus is then applied back to farmland, which enriches the soil and reduces the need for harmful fertilizers.

## Academic Talk — Guerrilla marketing (4번)

(N) Listen to a talk in a marketing class.
(W-Am) Today we’ll be discussing an innovative but somewhat unorthodox marketing concept. I’m talking about guerrilla marketing. In a nutshell, guerrilla marketing is an advertising strategy that focuses on low-cost, unconventional marketing tactics that yield maximum results. It’s been around for quite a while—since the 1980s, in fact.
Now, one of the key aspects of guerrilla marketing is creativity. Unlike traditional marketing, which often requires large budgets for TV commercials or print ads, guerrilla marketing relies on imaginative ideas to capture public attention. Think of flash mobs, viral videos, and street art. Using these tactics to promote your products can create buzz and engage audiences in ways that more conventional methods might not.
Another important element is the use of unexpected locations or events. For instance, a company might project a huge image of its product onto the side of a building...or organize a surprise, pop-up event in a crowded public space where the product takes center stage. These surprise elements can generate word-of-mouth publicity and are often shared widely on social media, amplifying their impact.
However, guerrilla marketing is not without its challenges. Because it relies on public spaces and unexpected events, it can sometimes face lawsuits or public backlash if not executed carefully. In addition, measuring the success of guerrilla marketing campaigns can be difficult. They don’t always translate directly into sales figures.

## Academic Reading — Elephants (1·5번 오답)

Elephants are known for their complex social structures and behaviors. Female elephants lead matriarchal herds, which include their offspring and related females. These groups are cohesive and display cooperative behaviors. For example, when a member of the herd is injured, others assist and care for it. This level of cooperation is rare among terrestrial mammals and highlights elephants' intelligence and empathy.

Communication within herds is vital for maintaining bonds. Elephants use vocalizations, such as trumpets and low-frequency rumbles, to convey information. These sounds can travel long distances, allowing communication even when far apart. At closer ranges, elephants use body language, like trunk gestures and ear movements, to express emotions and intentions.

An intriguing aspect of elephant behavior is mourning rituals.When a herd member dies, elephants engage in activities resembling mourning. They often stand vigil over the body, touch it gently with their trunks, and cover it with branches. This behavior suggests a deep emotional connection and an understanding of death that is unusual in the animal kingdom.

## Academic Reading — Deep-sea exploration (5번 오답, 문항 화면 없음)

Deep-sea exploration has expanded our understanding of the ocean's depths, revealing diverse and often bizarre organisms. The deep sea begins at around 200 meters below the surface and is characterized by high pressure, low temperatures, and complete darkness. Despite this harsh environment, life thrives in various forms.

One remarkable discovery is hydrothermal vents. ■ These fissures in the ocean floor release heated water rich in minerals, creating unique ecosystems. ■ Organisms near hydrothermal vents, such as tube worms and giant clams, have cell membranes and proteins that are adapted to remain functional under crushing pressure. ■ These organisms also rely on chemosynthesis--a process where bacteria convert the vent's chemicals into energy-rather than photosynthesis. ■ Living in perpetual darkness, vent organisms also do not rely on vision, although vent shrimp can detect faint infrared from vent emissions.

Advancements in technology have enabled scientists to explore deeper parts of the vehicles (ROVs) and submersibles equipped with cameras and sensors can withstand the pressure and capture images of deep-sea creatures. These explorations have uncovered new species and provided insights into how life can exist under such extreme conditions. However, deep-sea exploration remains challenging and costly, Funding and international collaboration are essential for continuing research.
